Lithium dihydrogen phosphate plant design

CHEE 456 Design Project · Team of four · Fall 2026

My design team is evaluating a plant that would produce 30,000 tonnes per year of battery-grade lithium dihydrogen phosphate (LDP), a precursor for LFP and LMFP battery cathodes, from the lithium-rich eluate of a direct lithium extraction process. We compared four production routes (lithium carbonate, lithium hydroxide, lithium phosphate and direct-to-LDP) using trade-off studies and key performance indicators, then evaluated the technologies for each step of the selected route, including hardness removal, polishing, concentration, precipitation, conversion to LDP, and solid-liquid separation, washing and drying. The final design aims to maximize lithium recovery while minimizing capital cost, operating cost, water, reagents and waste.

The role of kinesin in biotechnology

CHEE 370 Elements of Biotechnology · Team of four · Fall 2024

With three teammates, I produced a research poster on kinesin, the ATP-powered motor protein that moves cargo along microtubules. It covers the protein's structure and function, how its activity is regulated through autoinhibition, activation and phosphorylation, and its applications in cell biology, cancer therapy and nanotechnology. The poster also lays out how kinesin is produced, from plasmid and vector selection to expression in a host organism, submerged fermentation, and formulation as a liquid buffer or freeze-dried powder.

Calibrating a thermistor to measure cooling water

CHEE 291 Instrumentation and Measurement · Fall 2023

I built a temperature sensor from a 10 kΩ thermistor, a voltage divider and an Arduino Uno, then calibrated it against ice, tap and boiling water baths. The logarithmic calibration curve fit the three reference points with R² = 0.9975. I used the sensor to record 30 minutes of cooling for 500 mL of boiling water in an insulated thermos and in a ceramic mug, and fit Newton's law of cooling to the data. The cooling constant was 0.00034 s⁻¹ for the thermos and 0.00056 s⁻¹ for the mug, as predicted. The report also covers error analysis and repeatability.
